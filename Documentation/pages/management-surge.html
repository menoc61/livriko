Surge Pricing

Surge pricing automatically increases ride fares when demand exceeds the supply of available drivers. This time-based pricing model helps balance supply and demand, incentivizes drivers to go online during peak hours, and ensures ride availability when riders need it most.

How Surge Pricing Works

Understanding the mechanics of demand-based pricing.

Surge pricing in Livriko is configured as time-based rules that apply to specific days and time windows. When a surge rule is active, a multiplier is applied to all ride fares within the rule's parameters.

The Surge Flow

  1. Rule activation — The system checks the current time and day against configured surge rules. If the current time falls within a rule's window, the surge multiplier is applied.
  2. Rider notification — Riders see the surge multiplier displayed in the app before confirming their ride. A "Surge Pricing Active" indicator appears on the ride selection screen.
  3. Fare calculation — The base fare (base amount + vehicle charge + distance + time charges) is multiplied by the surge factor. For example, a $10 base fare with a 1.5x surge becomes $15.
  4. Driver earnings — Drivers earn the full surge-adjusted fare minus the platform commission, incentivizing more drivers to go online during peak periods.

Surge Multiplier Examples

Base Fare: $10.00

1.2x Surge → $12.00 (20% increase)
1.5x Surge → $15.00 (50% increase)
2.0x Surge → $20.00 (100% increase / double fare)
2.5x Surge → $25.00 (150% increase)

Creating Surge Rules

Follow these steps to set up time-based surge pricing.

1. Navigate to Surge Pricing

Log in to the admin panel. Go to Livriko Management → Surge Pricing. You will see a list of existing surge rules with their active status.

2. Click "Add New Rule"

Click the Add New Rule button to open the surge rule creation form.

3. Set Time Window

Configure the start time and end time for the surge rule. Use 24-hour format (HH:MM). For example, set 07:00 to 09:00 for a morning rush hour surge.

4. Select Days

Choose which days of the week the surge rule applies to. Use the multi-select checkboxes to select one or more days. Select all weekdays for a commuter rush hour rule, or specific days for weekend nightlife surges.

5. Save and Activate

Set the Status to Active and click Save. The surge rule will automatically apply during the configured time windows.

Field Reference

Complete reference for every field on the surge rule form.

Field Type Required Description
Start Time Time (HH:MM) Yes The time of day when the surge multiplier begins to apply. Use 24-hour format. For example, 07:00 for 7:00 AM or 17:00 for 5:00 PM.
End Time Time (HH:MM) Yes The time of day when the surge multiplier stops applying. Use 24-hour format. If the end time is earlier than the start time (e.g., start 22:00, end 03:00), the rule wraps around midnight.
Days Multi-select Yes Days of the week when the surge rule is active. Select one or more days: Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday, Sunday. At least one day must be selected.
Status Toggle Yes Active enables the surge rule during the configured time windows. Inactive disables it without deleting the rule. Useful for temporarily pausing surge during promotions or holidays.

How Multipliers Work

Detailed explanation of surge multiplier calculation and application.

Multiplier Application

The surge multiplier is applied to the total ride fare before commission and tax are deducted. The calculation flow is:

1. Base Fare = Base Amount + Vehicle Charge
2. Distance Charge = Distance × Per-KM Rate
3. Time Charge = Duration × Per-Minute Rate
4. Subtotal = Base Fare + Distance Charge + Time Charge
5. Surge Amount = Subtotal × (Surge Multiplier - 1)
6. Total Fare = Subtotal + Surge Amount
7. Commission = Total Fare × Commission %
8. Tax = Total Fare × Tax %
9. Driver Earnings = Total Fare - Commission + Tax

Overlapping Rules

If multiple surge rules overlap (e.g., a morning rush from 07:00-09:00 and a general peak rule from 06:00-10:00), the highest applicable multiplier is used. Surge multipliers do not stack or multiply together.

Rider Transparency
Riders always see the surge multiplier before confirming a ride. This transparency builds trust and reduces disputes. The surge amount is clearly displayed in the fare breakdown.

Midnight Wrap-Around

When the start time is later than the end time (e.g., start: 22:00, end: 04:00), the surge rule wraps around midnight and applies from 10:00 PM to 4:00 AM the next day. This is useful for nightlife and late-night surge pricing.

Revenue Optimization Tips

Strategies for using surge pricing effectively without alienating riders.

Start Conservative
Begin with modest multipliers (1.2x-1.5x) and increase gradually based on market response. Aggressive surge pricing (2.5x+) can drive riders to competitors and damage brand perception.

Effective Surge Strategies

  • Rush hour surges — Configure 1.2x-1.5x for weekday morning (07:00-09:00) and evening (17:00-19:00) rush hours. These are predictable demand peaks.
  • Weekend nightlife — Apply 1.3x-1.8x surges on Friday and Saturday nights (22:00-03:00) when bar and restaurant traffic peaks.
  • Event-based surges — Create temporary surge rules for concerts, sports events, and festivals. Remember to deactivate them after the event.
  • Weather-responsive — During rain or extreme weather, surge helps attract more drivers to meet increased demand.

What to Avoid

  • Excessive multipliers — Multipliers above 2.5x cause significant rider dissatisfaction and may lead to regulatory scrutiny in some markets.
  • Too many overlapping rules — Keep your surge rules simple and clear. Complex configurations are harder to manage and explain to riders.
  • Ignoring driver supply — Surge pricing only works if there are enough drivers to meet demand. Monitor driver availability alongside surge rules.
Regulatory Considerations
Some jurisdictions regulate or cap surge pricing multipliers. Check local transportation regulations before implementing surge pricing in a new market. Some cities require advance disclosure of maximum surge multipliers.

Monitoring & Analytics

Track surge performance to optimize your pricing strategy.

Key Metrics to Watch

  • Surge frequency — How often surge activates in each zone. Frequent surge may indicate a need for more drivers in that area.
  • Average multiplier — The typical surge level during peak periods. Track this over time to identify trends.
  • Rider drop-off rate — Percentage of riders who see the surge notification but cancel their booking. High drop-off suggests multipliers are too aggressive.
  • Driver online rate during surge — More drivers should go online when surge is active. If they don't, the surge may not be compelling enough.
  • Revenue per ride during surge — Compare average fare during surge vs. non-surge periods to measure revenue impact.
Data-Driven Adjustments
Review surge analytics weekly. If a particular time slot consistently shows high rider drop-off, reduce the multiplier. If surge rarely activates, the time windows may need adjustment to match actual demand patterns.
