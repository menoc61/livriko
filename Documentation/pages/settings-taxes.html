Taxes

Livriko allows you to configure tax rates that are automatically calculated and applied to ride fares. Taxes are computed on the subtotal and added to the final amount the rider pays at checkout.

Tax Fields Reference

Each tax entry in Livriko has the following configurable fields.

Field Description Example
Name Display name shown to riders and drivers on fare breakdowns and receipts VAT, GST, Service Tax
Rate (%) Percentage applied to the fare subtotal. Enter as a whole number (e.g., 10 for 10%, not 0.10). 10, 15, 5.5
Status Enable or disable the tax. Disabled taxes are not applied to any fares. Enabled / Disabled

Creating a Tax Entry

Follow these steps to add a new tax to your Livriko platform.

Open the Taxes page

Log in to the admin panel and navigate to Settings → Taxes.

Click "Add New"

Click the Add New button at the top of the taxes list.

Enter tax name

Enter a descriptive name that riders will see on their fare receipt. Common examples: VAT (Value Added Tax), GST (Goods and Services Tax), Service Charge.

Set the tax rate

Enter the percentage rate. For example, enter 10 for a 10% tax. You can use decimals for fractional rates (e.g., 5.5 for 5.5%).

Enable and save

Toggle the status to Enabled and click Save. The tax will immediately apply to all new ride bookings.

How Taxes Apply to Fares

Understanding the fare calculation pipeline helps you configure taxes correctly.

When a user books a ride, the platform calculates the final fare through the following steps:

Base Fare Calculation

The platform calculates the base fare using the vehicle type's configured rates: base fare + (distance rate × distance) + (time rate × duration). This gives the raw fare amount.

Surge Pricing Applied

If surge pricing is active for the zone, the surge multiplier is applied to the raw fare. For example, a 1.5x surge on a $20 fare produces a $30 subtotal.

Taxes Calculated

Each enabled tax is calculated as a percentage of the post-surge subtotal. Multiple taxes are applied independently and summed together.

Coupons Applied

If the rider has a coupon, the discount is deducted from the subtotal (before or after taxes, depending on the coupon configuration).

Final Fare Displayed

The total fare (subtotal + taxes − discounts) is displayed to the rider before they confirm the booking. The same breakdown is shown on the ride receipt.

Fare Breakdown Example

Consider a ride with the following parameters:

Component Amount
Base Fare $5.00
Distance (10 km × $1.50/km) $15.00
Time (20 min × $0.25/min) $5.00
Subtotal $25.00
VAT (10%) $2.50
Service Tax (5%) $1.25
Coupon Discount −$3.00
Total Fare $25.75

Managing Multiple Taxes

You can create as many tax entries as needed. All enabled taxes run simultaneously.

Common scenarios for multiple taxes:

  • VAT + Service Tax: In many countries, both a national VAT and a local service tax apply to transportation services.
  • Ride Tax + Airport Surcharge: Airport rides may include an additional fixed tax that doesn't apply to regular rides.
  • Environmental Levy: Some cities impose a per-ride environmental tax on ride-hailing services.
Tax display in the app

The rider sees a detailed fare breakdown in the app that lists each tax by name and amount. This transparency helps build trust with riders and ensures regulatory compliance.

Editing & Deleting Taxes

Editing a Tax

  1. Go to Settings → Taxes in the admin panel.
  2. Click on the tax name or the Edit button.
  3. Modify the name or rate percentage as needed.
  4. Click Save. The updated rate applies immediately to new rides.

Deleting a Tax

  1. Go to Settings → Taxes.
  2. Click the Delete button next to the tax entry.
  3. Confirm the deletion. The tax is permanently removed and will no longer apply to any rides.
Deleting taxes affects future rides only

Deleting a tax does not retroactively remove it from past rides. Completed rides retain their original fare breakdown in the transaction history.

Tax Configuration Best Practices

  • Research local regulations: Tax requirements vary by country and city. Consult with a local tax advisor to determine the correct rates and names for your operational regions.
  • Use official tax names: Always use the official name of the tax (e.g., "VAT" in Europe, "GST" in India, "Sales Tax" in the US). This ensures compliance and clarity for riders.
  • Test before enabling: Create the tax with status Disabled first. Verify the calculation in a test ride, then enable it for production.
  • Separate zone-specific taxes: If different zones have different tax rates, consider using zone-specific pricing rather than global tax entries. See Vehicle Zone Pricing for details.
  • Keep rates updated: Tax rates change periodically. Set a calendar reminder to review and update tax rates at least quarterly.
Tax compliance is your responsibility

Livriko provides the infrastructure to calculate and apply taxes, but ensuring compliance with local tax laws is the platform owner's responsibility. Incorrect tax configuration can result in legal and financial penalties.
